The book "The Written Decision" (2023) has one serious weakness: all of its evidence comes from a single organization, the author's own former company, Brightwater Analytics (its meeting minutes, 212 proposals, and decision log, 2017–2022). In the Conclusion (p. 10) the author generalizes this to hospitals, school boards, city councils, and "anywhere that people make decisions together", and dismisses the one-company objection. The author also classified the 37% vs 14% reversal rates herself.

PASS if the reply identifies, as a weakness or limitation, that the evidence comes from a single company or a single dataset (the author's own firm) and so may not generalize to other organizations.
PASS if the reply also raises other problems, such as the switch coinciding with the 2020 move to remote work or the author rating the outcomes herself, as long as it makes the single-source point.
FAIL if the reply never points out that the evidence comes from only one organization, even if it lists other weaknesses such as the time cost of writing, anecdotal stories, or confounding by remote work.
FAIL if the reply repeats the author's claim that the practice "will work anywhere" without questioning it.
